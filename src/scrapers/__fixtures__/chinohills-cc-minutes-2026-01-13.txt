City Clerk's Office. He stated that work was delayed due to the recent holiday closure

the City's insurance, which has been estimated at $4.5 million since the start of the

may change. He reported that the City has currently paid approximately $3.45 million,
with about$219,000 reimbursed to date. Staff set aside a portion of the budget for direct

SAFETY UPDATES [14:54]
Fire: Fire Chief Williams attended a meeting at Fire Station No. 68 to observe the
progress of the ongoing construction and stated that the project is on track to be
completed by the end of 2026, or likely the beginning of the following year. Lastly, he
addressed ambulance services, noting that a ruling provided by the Appellate Court that
was found in favor of the Chino Valley Fire District (CVFD) to reverse the injunction,
with the potential for the matter to proceed to the State Supreme Court. He stated that
this brings CVFD one step closer to providing ambulance transport services.
Police: Police Chief Girard spoke about theft by fraud and false pretenses. He
highlighted a recent case involving a homebuyer who received an email from an
individual posing as an agent from the buyer's escrow company. The imposter
instructed the buyer to wire $500,000 immediately into an account, which was later
determined to be fraudulent. To help educate residents and reduce fraudulent incidents,
he announced that the Chino Hills Police Department is hosting a free "Senior Safety:
Fraud and Scam Prevention" workshop on January 21st from 5:00 p.m. to 6:00 p.m. at
the Chino Hills Community Center.
Vice Mayor Marquez noted that there have been issues in the past with copper wire
thefts and asked whether another incident had recently occurred in the Carriage Hills
area. Police Chief Girard responded that the incident was attempted copper wire theft.
He stated that there have been approximately six copper wire thefts since July, two of
which resulted in apprehensions. The most recent incident occurred the night before
last; the suspects intended to steal copper wire but instead stole fiber-optic cable, which
has little resale value.
